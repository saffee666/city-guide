Chengdu

A city that turns daily life into a holiday

🏯Must-See6 top sights🗺️Route3-day itinerary🥢Food5 local picks🛏️StayWhere to base💡TipsBooking & pitfalls

A city that turns daily life into a holiday — pandas, teahouses, hotpot and ancient Shu wonders

A city that turns daily life into a holiday. Covered-bowl tea, hotpot, pandas and greenways — the pace is so slow you won't want to rush. Chengdu's charm isn't in its sights, but in its ease.

How to Get There

Chengdu has two international airports (Tianfu TFU and Shuangliu CTU) and is a high-speed rail hub, reaching Chongqing, Xi'an, Kunming and beyond.

Show More Information

Fly into Tianfu or Shuangliu (both linked by metro) or arrive at Chengdu East and South stations. The metro plus ride-hailing cover the city; the Panda Base is on the metro, while Sanxingdui and Dujiangyan are day trips by tour or high-speed train.

  • See the roly-poly pandas at the panda base

  • Find a hole-in-the-wall for authentic Sichuan food

Must-See Attractions

Chengdu's essential sights — with a practical tip for each.

Giant Panda Breeding Base

Watch adorable giant pandas munch bamboo and tumble about in the early morning — the earlier you go, the more active they are.

Tip: Go right at opening (around 8am) when pandas are most active and feeding — book ahead. To see star panda 'Hua Hua', expect a long, separate queue.

Kuanzhai Alley

Qing-dynasty lanes turned leisure district — tea houses, ear-cleaning and snacks, the model of laid-back old Chengdu.

Tip: Qing-era lanes of tea houses, ear-cleaning and snacks — touristy but fun; pair it with People's Park for the real teahouse vibe.

Jinli & Wuhou Shrine

A Three Kingdoms-themed old street and shrine — red lanterns, opera stages and chuan-chuan skewers.

Tip: A Three Kingdoms shrine and themed old street — red lanterns and chuan-chuan; liveliest in the evening.

People's Park · Heming Teahouse

Bamboo chairs, covered-bowl tea, ear-cleaning and mahjong — the most authentic Chengdu afternoon.

Tip: The most authentic Chengdu afternoon — bamboo chairs, covered-bowl tea, ear-cleaning and mahjong. Free to enter; just buy a tea.

Dujiangyan

An ancient irrigation marvel still working after 2,000+ years; the Min River splits where engineering meets landscape. (Nearby)

Tip: A 2,000-year-old irrigation marvel about 1h out — engineering meets landscape; combine with Mount Qingcheng for a day trip.

Du Fu Thatched Cottage

A garden built on the poet-sage's former home — bamboo groves, flowing water and quiet poetry.

Tip: A serene garden on the poet Du Fu's former home — red walls, bamboo and water; a calm, photogenic break.

An efficient first-timer's loop, grouped by theme and split into morning, afternoon and evening.

Where to Eat

Well-loved Chengdu tables — popular spots, so reserve ahead or expect a wait at peak times.

Chengdu Hotpot Must-do

Numbing-spicy beef-tallow hotpot — the city's headline meal; order mild if you're new to it.

Chuan-Chuan & Maocai Street

Skewers and bowl-served 'pick-your-own' spicy broth — casual Chengdu comfort food.

Laoma Pig-Trotter Soup Local

Silky stewed trotters in a mild, collagen-rich soup — a Chengdu late-night favourite.

Egg Pancake (Danhonggao) Snack

Little folded egg cakes with sweet or savoury fillings — a beloved street snack.

Bobo Chicken Cold dish

Skewered cold bites bathed in a fragrant chilli-and-sesame sauce.

Where to Stay

Downtown

Chunxi Road / Taikoo Li

Central shopping, dining and metro — the easy first-timer base.

Old-town charm

Kuanzhai Alley / People's Park

Near the teahouses and lanes, central and atmospheric.

Best value

Along the metro

Anywhere on the metro gives cheap, fast access — including straight to the Panda Base line.

Tips & Pitfalls

🐼 Pandas, go early

Arrive at opening (~8am) for active pandas; star panda 'Hua Hua' has a long separate queue.

📅 Book ahead

Reserve the Panda Base and Sanxingdui online; both get busy.

🚇 Getting around

Metro plus ride-hailing cover the city; Sanxingdui and Dujiangyan are day trips by train or tour.

🌶️ Tame the spice

Hotpot is fierce — order a mild or split pot and pace the chillies.

Best Time to Visit

Chengdu is mild much of the year; spring and autumn are best. Summers are hot and humid, winters cool, grey and often overcast — but the city's slow teahouse charm works in any season.

Local Specialties

Hotpot Base

Boxed Sichuan hotpot and maocai bases to take the heat home.

Shu Embroidery

One of China's four great embroidery styles, with panda and flower motifs.

Panda Goods

Endless adorable panda toys, tea tins and creative gifts.

Sichuan Tea & Brocade

Mengding and jasmine teas, and Shu brocade — classic Chengdu gifts.
